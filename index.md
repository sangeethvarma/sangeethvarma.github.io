I'm a PhD candidate in the School of Social Sciences at the National Institute of Advanced Studies (NIAS), Bengaluru, working under the supervision of Dr. Anant Kamath. My research sits at the intersection of Science and Technology Studies, political ecology, and development studies — broadly, I'm interested in how knowledge, expertise, and infrastructure shape (and are shaped by) contested visions of progress.

My doctoral work, tentatively titled *"Developmental Publics: Infrastructure, Politics, and Contested Visions of Progress in Keralam,"* looks at how Kerala's much-studied development trajectory has taken an "infrastructure turn" over the last two decades — a shift toward capital-intensive megaprojects financed through mechanisms like the Kerala Infrastructure Investment Fund Board (KIIFB). Using the shelved K-Rail SilverLine project as an empirical site, I'm trying to understand how what I call "Developmental Publics" — the multiple, shifting collectivities that form around competing ideas of infrastructure, welfare, ecology, and progress — negotiate, contest, and reshape state-led development, and what this reveals about the place of expertise and democratic participation in development planning today.

I didn't arrive here by the obvious route. I trained first in physics and electrical engineering at BITS Pilani, working in a quantum transport lab on the automation of multi-gated device measurements — work that led to a [publication on 2D superconductivity](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-018-0091-7). Somewhere in there I found myself more drawn to the social and political questions beneath the technical ones — how knowledge gets made, contested, and put to use — which eventually took me through a Master's in Political Science (IGNOU) and then Development Studies at TISS Mumbai, where my dissertation examined the Kerala Shastra Sahitya Parishad, a people's science movement, and how it responded to the COVID-19 pandemic.

Along the way I've also worked on climate-smart agriculture policy at the Council on Energy, Environment and Water (CEEW), and on community well-being and multidimensional poverty surveys with YUVA India.
